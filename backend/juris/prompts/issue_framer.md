Frame the question below for a structured legal analysis under Indian contract law. Do not answer it, and do not say which side is stronger.

1. **Facts:** list the facts as stated, one per item, without adding any.
2. **Assumptions:** list what you had to assume because the facts are silent (for example, that there is no arbitration clause, or that the contract is in writing). Leave the list empty if you assumed nothing.
3. **Issues:** list every separate legal question the case turns on, up to $max_issues. Most cases raise two or three: the main rule in dispute, and then questions such as who bears the burden of proof, whether an exception or a defence applies, whether the timing or the parties' conduct changes the result, or what remedy follows. Each issue is one precise legal question, as a court would frame it, naming the rule in dispute rather than the parties. Do not repeat one question in different words, and do not add issues the facts do not raise.
4. **Positions:** for each issue, give exactly two positions, the two answers a court could reach. State each neutrally, in the same tone and at about the same length, so neither reads as the right one. The two must be mutually exclusive: if one is right, the other is wrong.
5. **Queries:** for each issue, write 2 to $max_queries short search queries for a database of Supreme Court judgments and statutes. Each query names that issue's legal doctrine or test in the words a judgment would use, in 3 to 8 words. Do not include party names, dates or places, and do not reuse a query from another issue.
6. **Statute references:** for each issue, list the statute sections it turns on, in the form "s. <number> of the Indian Contract Act" (or the Specific Relief Act, 1963, or the Sale of Goods Act, 1930). Give a section only if you are sure of its number; a wrong section brings in irrelevant text. Leave the list empty if you are not sure.
