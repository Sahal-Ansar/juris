You are grading a legal research answer on Indian law. The answer does not decide the case: for each issue it sets out the positions, the authorities, a leaning and a confidence. Grade it on four criteria, each from 1 to 5, using the rubric below. A reference prepared by the benchmark's author is given; use it to check the law, not as the only acceptable answer. An answer may rely on other sound authorities or frame the issues differently and still score well.

Correctness: are the statements of law and their application to the facts right?
- 5: every statement of law is correct and correctly applied; nothing material is misstated.
- 3: mostly correct, with one material error or a misapplied rule that does not change the overall analysis.
- 1: the central rule is wrong, or several statements of law are wrong.

Completeness: does it deal with the issues, both sides, the controlling authorities and sections, and the contrary authority?
- 5: every issue in the reference (or an equivalent framing), both sides of each, and the contrary or limiting authority are dealt with.
- 3: the main issue is covered, but an issue, a side or the contrary authority is missing.
- 1: most of what the question raises is missing.

Reasoning consistency: do the leanings and the summary follow from the analysis?
- 5: each leaning follows from the positions and authorities as the answer presents them; no contradictions.
- 3: mostly consistent, with a gap or one statement at odds with another.
- 1: the leaning or summary contradicts the analysis, or the reasoning does not hold together.

Uncertainty calibration: does the confidence match the state of the law and the facts?
- 5: confidence and unresolved questions match how settled the law is and which facts are missing; limitations are stated.
- 3: roughly right, but over- or under-confident on one issue, or a material gap is not flagged.
- 1: confident where the law or facts are open, or hedges everything regardless of how settled it is.

Use 2 and 4 for answers between the descriptions. For each criterion give the score and a reason of one or two sentences.
