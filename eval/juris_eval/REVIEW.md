## JE-001 (dev, medium): undue influence

**Question.** Can a gift deed executed by a very old man in favour of his grandson be set aside for undue influence merely because of their close relationship and the donor's age?

**Facts.** A man in his late eighties executed a registered gift deed transferring part of his land to his only grandson, who lived with him. After his death, his sons sued to cancel the deed, pleading that given the relationship and the donor's great age the court should presume undue influence and put the grandson to proof that the gift was the donor's free act. There was no evidence that the donor was of unsound mind or that the grandson controlled his affairs.

**Issues.**
- Does a close family relationship between donor and donee, or the donor's old age, by itself raise a presumption of undue influence under s. 16 of the Contract Act?
- On whom does the burden of proof lie, and when does it shift to the donee?

**Sections.** contract_act:16, contract_act:19A

**Key points.**
- Section 16(1) requires both a position to dominate the will and use of that position to obtain an unfair advantage.
- Relationship or age alone does not raise a presumption; s. 16(2) lists situations where domination may be found.
- The burden shifts under s. 16(3) only once domination is shown and the transaction appears unconscionable.
- Fiduciary relationships and extreme age or infirmity can shift the onus (the contrary line of cases).

## JE-002 (test, medium): coercion and economic duress

**Question.** Is an insured bound by a "full and final" discharge voucher it signed when the insurer refused to release even the admitted amount without it?

**Facts.** After a fire, the insurer's surveyor assessed the loss at far less than the insured's claim. The insurer said it would pay the assessed amount only if the insured first signed an undated voucher accepting it in full and final settlement. Short of funds, the insured signed, received the money and immediately protested that it had signed under coercion. It now seeks arbitration of the balance under the policy's arbitration clause.

**Issues.**
- Does a discharge voucher obtained by withholding admitted dues amount to coercion or economic duress that vitiates the settlement?
- Can the insured still refer the balance claim to arbitration after signing the voucher?

**Sections.** contract_act:14, contract_act:15, contract_act:19

**Key points.**
- Consent obtained by coercion is not free (s. 14); the agreement is voidable at the option of the coerced party (s. 19).
- Indian courts recognise economic duress, e.g. withholding admitted dues to extract a full and final discharge.
- A disputed discharge voucher does not by itself extinguish the arbitrable dispute.
- The plea must be credible and is decided on evidence in the arbitration.

## JE-006 (test, medium): wagering agreements

**Question.** Can a partner recover his share of losses from a partnership formed to enter into wagering transactions?

**Facts.** Two traders agreed to share profits and losses of forward contracts in grain that both knew would be settled only by paying differences, never by delivery. The contracts made a loss, which one partner paid to the counterparties. He sued the other for half. The defendant argued that the partnership was itself unlawful because its object was wagering.

**Issues.**
- Are wagering agreements void or illegal under the Contract Act?
- Is an agreement collateral to a wager (a partnership to carry on wagers) unlawful under s. 23?

**Sections.** contract_act:30, contract_act:23

**Key points.**
- Section 30 makes wagers void (unenforceable), not illegal.
- Void is not the same as forbidden by law; collateral agreements survive unless another law forbids them.
- Public policy under s. 23 is not extended to new heads merely because wagering is disapproved.
- The partner can recover the share of the losses.

**Supporting: Gherulal Parakh v. Mahadeodas Maiya**, [1959] Supp 2 SCR 406 (`SC-S_1959_2_406_447`)

Proposition: A wagering contract is void under s. 30 but not forbidden by law, so an agreement collateral to it, such as a partnership to carry on wagering transactions, is not unlawful under s. 23.
